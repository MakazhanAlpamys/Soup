E3 — LoRA versus LoRA-FA at longer sequences
Research report, 2026-10-15. Ready for review; formal acceptance pending.

Result and recommendation
For the tested Qwen2.5-0.5B Soup RAM-streaming recipe on RTX3090, retain ordinary
LoRA as the default. LoRA-FA increased mean arm-median step time by 11.24%, 8.39%
and 6.11% at sequence lengths 512, 2048 and 4096. Its optimizer state was smaller,
but peak PyTorch allocated memory fell by only 5.25 MiB at each length. Short-run
held-out loss was higher with FA. These observations do not establish behavior
for other targets/ranks/models or converged training. FA remains worth evaluating
when adapter optimizer storage is a demonstrated bottleneck; this recipe does
not show a practical total-memory or speed advantage.

Existing report reviewed
soup-rnd/benchmarks/gate-725-lorafa-operating-point.md (#725).
That report uses a tiny LLaMA microbenchmark and an analytic saved-activation
ratio. Its 50% parameter/Adam reduction concerns square projections. It explicitly
does not claim equivalent total VRAM savings, end-to-end speed or downstream
quality. Our non-square Qwen targets show an approximately 63.64% optimizer-state
byte reduction, which must not be described as a 63.64% GPU-memory reduction.

Design and validity
Five ABBA blocks per length; A=LoRA, B=LoRA-FA; fresh process per arm.
15 accepted blocks, 60 arms. All required F1 checks returned ok with the empirical
overlay resolver. Result hashes and process exit codes verified for all 60 arms.
Original unadapted block remains unknown and is excluded.
512/block04 excluded in full: foreign reads 4,688,121,856 bytes exceeded 1 GB.
Its first good arm is not recycled. block04-replacement01 replaces the whole block.
Original diagnostics and rejected records remain in the archive.
Mapping calibration: exact sdb read deltas 64/192/64 MiB, zero idle reads.
Controls: idle ok, own 128 MiB ok, foreign 1280 MiB void with exact accounting.
The resolver uses F1's disk_device injection interface; counters, thresholds,
required checks and original F1 code are unchanged. Boot and mount layout checked.
This establishes guest virtual-disk controls, not physical-host isolation.
Four processes were not inspectable; disk-wide bounds still apply within the guest.

Repeat rule
Pinned F1 repeats()/blocks_to_add() applied to arm median seconds.
Pooled sigma seconds: 512=0.000200608; 2048=0.003419831; 4096=0.016776379.
N=max(2,ceil(7.85*(sigma/(0.1*mean_A))^2)) gives 2 for each length.
The mandatory five-block pilot exceeds N; blocks_to_add=0 for all three.
No further repetitions are requested by this rule. It plans detection of a 10%
effect, not proof of significance for the smaller observed effects.

Formal protocol limitation
F1 METHOD requires committing the rule before the first arm. No commit was made
under the user's no-commit instruction. The separate protocol was recorded before
blocks02-05, after exploratory block01 was observed. Do not retrospectively call
this series preregistered or formally F1-compliant. Reviewer must accept this
deviation or request a prospectively committed confirmation series.
