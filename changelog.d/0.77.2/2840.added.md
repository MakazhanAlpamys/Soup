- **`training.prefix_tree`: training batches laid out as prefix trees (#2840).** Tokens that several examples begin with and that are not trained on (one system prompt, one tool schema, one document) are held once per row, with a 4D attention mask (a token attends to itself and its ancestors) and `position_ids` equal to the depth in the tree. Every token sees the context it had in its own example, so the loss and its gradient are those of the ordinary batch holding the same examples; the step runs the trees' tokens instead of the sum of the examples' lengths, and a row holds as many examples as fit `data.max_length`. A step is still at most `batch_size` rows of `data.max_length` tokens, so the option is allowed beside `stream_layers` (where `packing` and `multipack` are refused). Before the first step the trainer runs two or three training examples as an ordinary batch and as one prefix-tree row and refuses a model that does not reproduce the logits. `task: sft`, `modality: text`, single process, not the mlx backend; refused beside `packing`, `multipack`, `stream_loss_chunk`, `data.format: raft`, `data.replay` and `data.interleave`. An epoch has fewer optimizer steps (a step holds several examples), so compare runs by wall time or examples seen. Measured on an RTX 3050 Laptop 4 GB, layer-streamed NF4: Mistral-7B on tool calling with one 4-function schema goes from 0.400 to 1.404 examples/s at 512-token rows (3.109 and 3.768 at 1024 and 2048), with a held-out loss of 0.1340 against 0.1488 in 66 % of the training time; 2.4x on glaive-function-calling-v2 as published and 2.2x on dolly, which shares nothing. One card, one seed, held-out loss only; `benchmarks/gate-prefix-tree.md` has the tables and what is not explained.
