- **The layer-streaming VRAM pre-flight charges the cuBLAS workspaces a step holds (#1407).** `estimate_stream_peak_vram` claims to predict `torch.cuda.max_memory_allocated()`, but a streamed step holds two cuBLAS workspaces -- one for the forward's thread, one for autograd's CUDA thread, each drawing a handle from PyTorch's `thread_local` pool -- and the formula named no term for them. `STREAM_FIXED_SLACK_BYTES` cannot have been absorbing them: at 13,500,000 B it is smaller than a single 32 MiB workspace. On an RTX 5070 (cc 12.0, torch 2.14.0+cu130) the 2-layer streamed fixture predicted 13,934,816 B against a real DPO peak of 67,734,016 B (4.86x low); with `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG=:4096:1` the peak fell to 9,013,760 B, so 58,720,256 B of the miss -- exactly 2 x (32 - 4) MiB -- was the workspace alone.

The new term is 2 handles x the device's workspace size, mirroring PyTorch's own `parseChosenWorkspaceSize` (32 MiB for compute capability 9-12, else the documented `:4096:2:16:8` = 8,320 KiB) and honouring `CUBLAS_WORKSPACE_CONFIG`, which the estimate reads rather than mirroring a second knob. It is the FULL size, not a delta over the legacy one, because criterion 3 of #1407 asks the charge to match the allocator and only the full size does: shrinking to 4 MiB must move the prediction by exactly the 58,720,256 B the allocator moved. The pre-flight resolves it from the live device before the fit decision and names it in the panel, and off-CUDA it is 0 -- there is no workspace without a device.

**The `<1%` accuracy claim in `tests/test_v07203.py` is re-scoped.** That grid's peaks were read on an RTX 3050 (cc 8.6) with the same allocator counter, so they already CONTAIN two 8,320 KiB workspaces that the constant carried implicitly; naming the term double counts 16.3 MB there and moves every row to +1.04%..+6.57% over-prediction (worst row SmolLM2-135M B1 S256). The claim is therefore split rather than widened: the `<1%` band is kept against the formula without the term, and a new assertion pins the band with it, requires it to stay an over-prediction, and checks each row moves by exactly 2 x 8,320 KiB so the widening is arithmetic rather than a re-fit. The direction property on both grids is untouched and still charges the workspace. Re-deriving the constant with the workspace split out needs the RTX 3050 back to refit against, which this change does not have. CPU-tested coverage lives in `tests/test_issue1407_cublas_workspace.py` and `tests/test_issue1407_red_proof.py`, with a `@pytest.mark.gpu` class asserting against the live allocator. (#1593)
